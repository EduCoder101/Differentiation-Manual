Step 2

Who are you differentiating for?

This shapes the direction of everything we build.

Support and extension are different in kind, not just degree. Support is about access — reducing barriers, providing scaffolds, making content reachable. Extension is about depth — increasing abstraction, complexity, and autonomy. Knowing which you're designing for changes which levers to pull, and changes what a successful outcome looks like.
Students who need more support Focus on access, scaffolding, and reducing barriers
Students who need more extension Focus on depth, complexity, and autonomy
Both — I need two versions Produce a support and an extension version

The text

Surface readability — word choice, sentence length, lexile level — and conceptual complexity are different levers. Simplifying vocabulary doesn't automatically make a concept more accessible. Sometimes a concrete example or a well-chosen analogy does more work than a rewritten sentence. Both matter, but they need different approaches.

The questions

Select all that apply — or none if you only want to change the text.

Cognitive demand in questions follows Bloom's Taxonomy. Lower-order questions ask students to recall and describe. Higher-order questions ask students to analyse, evaluate, and create. Adjusting demand doesn't mean removing rigour — it means meeting students where they are and stretching from there.
