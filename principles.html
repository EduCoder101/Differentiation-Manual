Core Principles of Differentiation

Research-Based Foundations for Effective Differentiation

These nine principles represent the research-based foundations that guide effective differentiation. Each principle is grounded in decades of educational research and practical classroom experience. Understanding these principles will help you make informed decisions about when and how to differentiate instruction.

Anticipatory Differentiation

Use AI to interrogate your lessons from different student perspectives before you deliver them. Ask: What could be misconstrued? Where are the gaps? What's unclear? Where are likely misconceptions?

Why this matters: Catching potential barriers before instruction begins prevents students from struggling unnecessarily and allows you to build clarity from the start.

Research base: Tomlinson (2014) and other leading researchers define effective differentiation as being proactive rather than reactive. Instead of adjusting a lesson "on the fly" when students fail to understand, the anticipatory approach involves using deidentified class profiles and pre-assessment data to plan for diversity from the outset. This "stress-testing" ensures that lessons have built-in flexibility to accommodate varied learning speeds and readiness levels before they are even delivered.

Reactive Differentiation

Use your knowledge of students—their readiness levels, interests, and learning profiles—to shape instruction. Create deidentified class profiles that help AI tools understand your students' needs.

Why this matters: Generic differentiation helps no one. When you differentiate based on actual student data, you create materials that genuinely meet learners where they are.

Differentiation is Collaborative

Consult experts when needed

Click to learn more →

Collaborative Differentiation

For tier 2 and especially tier 3 students, differentiation works best when you consult with inclusive learning specialists who know the students well and can guide targeted interventions.

Why this matters: Complex learning needs require expert support. AI can help you create materials, but inclusive learning specialists provide the pedagogical insight that ensures those materials actually work.

Research base: Extensive studies show that teacher collaboration—particularly the "co-construction" of lessons with inclusive learning specialists—is significantly associated with more effective differentiated practices. Collaboration on Multi-Tiered Systems of Support (MTSS) ensures that interventions are targeted and data-driven, rather than isolated. Schools that implement weekly team meetings to assist teachers with individual student needs (such as the HPGE Team model) report higher teacher efficacy and more equitable student outcomes.

Quality Over Quantity

Differentiation isn't about giving advanced students "more work" or struggling students "less work." It's about adjusting the nature and complexity of the work to match readiness and challenge appropriately.

Why this matters: When we differentiate quantitatively (three problems vs. ten problems), we send the message that learning is about volume, not depth. When we differentiate qualitatively, we honour that all students deserve appropriately challenging, meaningful work.

Building Self-Reliant Learners

Effective differentiation helps students become aware of their own learning needs and develop the agency to meet them. It's not just about the teacher doing different things—it's about students learning to navigate their own learning.

Why this matters: The goal isn't perpetual dependence on teacher-created supports. It's helping students develop the skills to recognise when they need different approaches and how to access them.

Research base: John Hattie's meta-analysis identifies "Assessment-Capable Learners" (effect size 1.44) and "Self-Reported Grades" (effect size 1.33) as two of the most powerful influences on achievement. Differentiation builds this self-reliance by giving students agency—the power to make choices about their learning process. When students are taught to self-assess and understand their own learning profiles, they move from being passive recipients to independent managers of their own progress.

Differentiation is for Everyone

Not just struggling students

Click to learn more →

Differentiation for All Students

Differentiation isn't remediation in disguise. It's not only for "high" and "low" students or those who are disruptive. Every student benefits from instruction that matches their readiness, interests, and learning profile.

Why this matters: When we think of differentiation as only for struggling students, we miss opportunities to challenge and engage every learner. Students at all levels deserve materials that genuinely fit their needs.

Research insight: Studies show that when differentiation is implemented broadly rather than as intervention-only, all students show greater engagement and learning gains.

Equity Through Content Access

Foundation for all learning

Click to learn more →

Content Differentiation Creates Equity

Research consistently shows that differentiating content—how students access information—is the most powerful way to ensure equitable learning opportunities. When all students can access the core ideas, everything else becomes possible.

Why this matters: If a student can't access the content because it's too complex, too abstract, or presented in an inaccessible format, no amount of process or product differentiation will help. Content access is the foundation.

Research base: Tomlinson (2014), Subban (2006), and others emphasise that content differentiation is where equity truly lives in the classroom.

AI Builds Capacity

More choice, not more work

Click to learn more →

AI as Your Differentiation Partner

AI tools don't create more work—they create more capacity. They allow you to do the differentiation you've always known you should do but couldn't sustain. They handle the heavy cognitive lifting of creating multiple versions, freeing you to focus on pedagogical decisions.

Why this matters: Without AI, creating 3-5 versions of quality learning materials is unsustainable. With AI, you can create bespoke materials at scale, making personalisation actually feasible in a classroom of 20+ students.

Research insight: Modern literature highlights AI as a "pedagogical partner" that can solve the challenge of individualised education at scale. Generative AI enables sustainable differentiation by allowing teachers to create multiple versions of a text, levelled maths problems, or scaffolded prompts in minutes rather than hours. This technological support shifts AI from a tool for administrative efficiency to a capacity-builder, ensuring that high-quality, personalised instruction does not result in teacher burnout.

Differentiation is Data-Driven

Formative assessment guides the way

Click to learn more →

Data-Driven Differentiation

Effective differentiation is grounded in evidence about student learning—particularly formative assessment data gathered continuously throughout teaching. Quick checks, exit cards, observations, and discussions tell you what students know and what they need next.

Why this matters: Differentiation without data is just guessing. When you use ongoing formative assessment to guide your differentiation decisions, you're responding to actual student needs rather than assumptions. This is responsive teaching at its best.

Research insight: Studies consistently show that formative assessment—when used to inform instruction—has one of the highest effect sizes on student learning. It's not about summative testing; it's about gathering and using evidence as you teach.
